# Procedure Portfolio and Sequencing

## Selection criteria

Score candidate procedures on clinical value, task structure, anatomy variability,
visibility, tissue deformation, bleeding risk, energy use, instrument complexity,
availability of ground truth, conversion feasibility, data access, and regulator
feedback. The easiest dataset is not necessarily the safest clinical target.

## Recommended sequence

### Platform skills — no living subject

1. instrument identification, calibration, and tracking;
2. camera/endoscope positioning and field-of-view recovery;
3. motion scaling, tremor filtering, and virtual fixtures;
4. needle detection, grasping, regrasping, and handoff;
5. single suture throw and knot tightening on instrumented phantoms;
6. cutting along a marked path with protected boundaries;
7. suction/irrigation positioning in a simulated field;
8. emergency stop, release, withdrawal, and conventional conversion drills.

### Human track

Start with S1/S2 assistance in a narrow minimally invasive indication. Candidate
segments include camera control, navigation, port/trajectory guidance, and closure
assistance. Bounded S3 manipulation comes later and remains continuously
supervised. Procedure choice requires partner hospitals, specialty leadership,
data access, and regulator consultation.

### Dental track

1. CBCT/intraoral scan registration and anatomy segmentation;
2. implant-path planning with nerve/sinus/tooth safety margins;
3. shared-control alignment and depth stop on phantoms/extracted teeth;
4. clinician-supervised drilling in an authorized study;
5. only after separate evidence, bounded preparation or suturing segments.

### Veterinary track

Begin with one species, weight range, and procedure. Navigation/camera assistance
and closure tasks should precede tissue dissection. Each new species and body size
is a new anatomical population. Live-animal work is used only when justified,
ethically approved, and not replaceable by simulation, phantoms, cadavers, or
ex-vivo material.

## Excluded early targets

- emergency, trauma, or battlefield surgery;
- unsupervised or remote unattended surgery;
- autonomous anesthesia, medication, diagnosis, or consent;
- pediatric, fetal, intracranial, cardiac, transplant, or similarly high-consequence
  procedures before the platform has mature evidence;
- surgery in public or uncontrolled environments;
- autonomous response to uncontrolled hemorrhage or unexpected malignancy;
- cross-procedure or cross-species generalization without new validation.

## Procedure dossier template

Every candidate must have:

1. intended use, indication, contraindications, and target population;
2. users, environment, instruments, imaging, and accessories;
3. workflow and autonomy allocation by step;
4. critical anatomy, hazards, foreseeable misuse, and conversion triggers;
5. required data and ground truth;
6. simulator/phantom/cadaver or ex-vivo models and their limitations;
7. performance and safety endpoints with statistical analysis plan;
8. human-factors and team-training plan;
9. sterilization/reprocessing and service strategy;
10. regulatory pathway and postmarket monitoring;
11. contingency owner and conventional fallback.

## Promotion rule

A procedure segment advances only when its evidence is reproducible on the locked
configuration, all critical failures have detected responses, residual risks are
accepted by the appropriate authorities, and the next-stage protocol is approved.
Success in a demonstration, average metric, or synthetic environment alone is not
sufficient.
