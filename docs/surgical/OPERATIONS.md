# Surgical Operations and Team Model

## Minimum leadership

- chief medical officer and specialty clinical leads;
- dental and veterinary leads for their separate programs;
- anesthesia, nursing, sterile processing, infection prevention, and OR operations;
- systems, controls, mechanical, electrical, software, AI/data, and cybersecurity;
- safety engineering independent from feature delivery;
- quality, regulatory, clinical affairs, biostatistics, privacy, and legal;
- human factors, training, field service, manufacturing, and supplier quality;
- human-subject protection and animal-welfare expertise.

## Case roles

Assign before every investigational case:

- responsible clinician and qualified backup clinician;
- console/operator role and bedside assistant;
- anesthesia/physiology team independent of robot control;
- circulating and scrub roles;
- technical support with no authority over clinical decisions;
- safety monitor with stop authority;
- conversion leader and conventional equipment readiness owner;
- data/log custodian and adverse-event documentation owner.

## Pre-case readiness

1. confirm patient/procedure/site, consent, approvals, and inclusion criteria;
2. conduct the adapted surgical safety checklist and team briefing;
3. verify conventional fallback equipment, blood/hemostasis resources where
   applicable, and conversion roles;
4. verify sterile/reprocessed instruments, use life, accessories, and draping;
5. verify signed bundle, no Internet route, time, storage, logs, and rollback state;
6. verify robot, table, imaging, registration, calibration, limits, emergency stop,
   brakes, manual release, energy interlocks, and backup power;
7. rehearse expected checkpoints, alarms, takeover, abort, and conversion criteria;
8. begin motion only after clinician and team authorization.

Use an adapted WHO Surgical Safety Checklist with local clinical governance:
<https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/quality-of-care-and-patient-safety/patient-safety-guidance-and-tools/safe-surgery/tool-and-resources>.

## During the case

- Display active autonomy, controlling user, intended next action, limits, and
  confidence without obscuring the operative field.
- Require explicit authorization at procedure-segment boundaries.
- Use closed-loop verbal confirmation for takeover and conversion.
- Stop on stale registration, unknown instrument, lost visibility, safety-system
  disagreement, or unhandled physiologic/clinical change.
- Preserve the sterile field and clinical chain of command during technical faults.

## Post-case

- complete counts, device/instrument inspection, decontamination handoff, and log seal;
- record deviations, alarms, interventions, takeovers, conversions, adverse events,
  and outcome follow-up according to protocol;
- quarantine suspect instruments, bundles, or datasets;
- conduct clinical and technical debriefs without overwriting original records;
- assess reportability and whether enrollment/deployment must pause.

## Training and credentialing

Train each role on normal workflow, setup, limitations, alarm interpretation,
takeover, manual release, conversion, reprocessing, and incident reporting. Use
simulation-based proficiency criteria and periodic recertification. Experience on
one procedure, autonomy level, species, or configuration does not credential
another automatically.

## Maintenance and readiness

- scheduled inspection, calibration, brake/release tests, and preventive maintenance;
- instrument/accessory life and sterilization-cycle tracking;
- battery/UPS and emergency-power tests;
- offline vulnerability and dependency review;
- spare-part provenance and supplier change control;
- periodic restore, rollback, incident, and recall drills;
- case prohibition when service, calibration, training, or evidence is expired.
